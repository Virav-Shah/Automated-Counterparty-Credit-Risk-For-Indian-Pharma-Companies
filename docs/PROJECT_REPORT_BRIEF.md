## 3. How the P&L analysis was performed

Read revenue, EBITDA, EBIT and interest expense from the supplied five-year extract. Compare each issuer year by year before comparing peer ratios. Revenue growth measures expansion or contraction; EBITDA/EBIT growth and margin changes show whether growth is accompanied by stronger operating earnings. Interest coverage measures the earnings cushion against financing cost.

Useful calculations: revenue growth = current/prior revenue −1; EBITDA margin = EBITDA/revenue; EBIT margin = EBIT/revenue; interest coverage = EBIT/interest expense. FY21–FY25 CAGR has **four** annual intervals: `(FY25/FY21)^(1/4) −1`. Separate recurring operations from exceptional income, acquisitions and accounting changes when full statements become available.

Gross profit, detailed operating costs, depreciation, tax, exceptional items and PAT are not in the main financial extract. The report must not claim a full expense-quality, net-margin or earnings-quality reconciliation was completed from those missing fields. The difference EBITDA−EBIT is an arithmetic indicator only; do not assert that it is solely depreciation/amortization without statement notes.

## 4. How balance-sheet analysis was performed

Review total debt, cash, current assets, current liabilities, receivables and inventory. Compare debt to operating earnings and subtract cash to observe net leverage. Negative net debt is retained as a net-cash position. Current ratio tests broad short-term coverage but cannot establish that receivables and inventory are readily collectible/liquid.

Debt/EBITDA = total debt/EBITDA; net debt/EBITDA = (debt−cash)/EBITDA; current ratio = current assets/current liabilities. Compare receivable/inventory growth with sales growth to identify disproportionate working-capital accumulation. Follow up using ageing, impairment, inventory obsolescence, debt maturities and restricted-cash disclosures.

Receivable days need average receivables and compatible sales; inventory/payable days need compatible cost/purchase denominators and payables. The supplied extract lacks payables/cost of sales, so a complete cash conversion cycle has not been calculated. Equity/net worth, contingent liabilities and security details also need fuller statements for solvency/security analysis.

## 5. How cash-flow analysis was performed

Operating cash flow measures cash generated by operations; capex is deducted to calculate free cash flow. Compare OCF with EBITDA to screen cash conversion, and FCF with debt to assess financial flexibility. Positive earnings with weak OCF can reflect working-capital absorption; positive FCF can support debt reduction, but it is not all automatically available for creditors.

FCF = OCF−capex; FCF/debt = FCF/total debt; OCF/EBITDA is a cash-conversion screening ratio. Separate maintenance versus expansion capex, acquisition outlays, tax effects, restricted funds, dividends and financing flows when full cash-flow statements are available. The extract contains OCF and capex only: it does not establish that investing and financing cash flows were fully reconciled.

When debt or interest is zero, the related ratio is unavailable rather than infinite. Existing model policies explicitly address debt-free cash deficits and zero interest; describe these assumptions rather than treating missing denominators as an automatic external rating.

## 7. Internal credit-risk classification

The project’s 100-point **financial risk score** combines leverage (25), debt servicing (20), cash flow (20), liquidity (15), earnings resilience (10) and working-capital behavior (10). Higher points mean greater measured financial risk. Earnings and working-capital bands include proposed project rules; exact thresholds are documented in the methodology.

Bands are VERY LOW 0–14, LOW 15–29, MODERATE 30–49, HIGH 50–69 and CRITICAL 70–100. These labels are internal screening categories. They are not CRISIL/ICRA agency ratings, regulatory capital measures, probabilities of default or automatic credit approvals. FY21 has no full score because FY20 growth observations are absent; an 80-point observed core is shown separately.

The market stress score remains independent. It can increase review intensity, but a stock-price fall alone does not make an issuer a bad credit or change its primary financial band. Confirmed deterioration requires aligned fresh financial and market observations; the delivered FY25/October 2026 dates do not qualify.

## 8. Counterparty analysis and credit review

Counterparty analysis identifies the actual legal entity, financial capacity, business model, cash generation, liquidity and sources of risk before considering exposure. Parent/brand names do not establish which entity owes a receivable or guarantees an obligation.

A practical review memo should record: entity and ownership; latest statement dates/scope; financial ratios and score drivers; peer position; early-warning developments; narrative regulatory/customer/FX/business risks with dates; actual exposure; approved limit and terms; security/guarantees; analyst recommendation and approval. The project supplies ratios, financial bands, peer comparisons and review prompts, not a legally approved limit decision.

Credit reviews can be scheduled or event-driven. Triggers include leverage/coverage stress, negative FCF, liquidity pressure, debt acceleration, revenue decline, slower collections, score deterioration, market stress and effective entity transitions. Verify acquisitions, accounting changes and temporary effects before attributing causality. JB’s amalgamation prompts entity/exposure continuity review; historical JB ratios are not automatically successor ratios.

## 9. Credit-limit monitoring — proposed finance framework

**Current implementation gap:** approved limits, current exposure and utilization data have not been supplied. The final report must describe limit monitoring as a proposed extension/workflow, not claim that the dashboard has measured actual utilization or breaches.

Define exposure according to product: unpaid trade invoices and shipped-unbilled amounts for trade credit; outstanding principal, accrued obligations and policy-defined commitments for lending; product-specific valuation for other contracts. Avoid counting the same obligation twice. Track issuer/legal entity, group relationships, currency and snapshot date.

Core calculations: utilization = exposure/approved limit ×100; headroom = approved limit−exposure; breach = max(exposure−approved limit,0). A zero/missing limit is an exception requiring review, not a calculable healthy utilization rate. Distinguish gross exposure from eligible secured/net exposure; do not deduct collateral without valuation, enforceability, haircut and policy approval.

Suggested input fields: counterparty/legal-entity ID, group ID, date, exposure type, currency, approved limit, outstanding balance, committed/undrawn amount, overdue amount and ageing, collateral/guarantee, eligible value/haircut, limit expiry, review date, approver and exception status. Assign warning/breach bands only after a documented policy; proposed thresholds are not approved limits.

Review actions can include obtaining updated statements/collections evidence, reconciling exposure, verifying security, reviewing payment terms or recommending a limit change to the authorized decision-maker. The report should never equate a financial risk score with a specific rupee credit limit without an exposure methodology and approval policy.

## 11. Findings and limitations to emphasize

The active extract yields 95 company-year observations and 76 full financial scores. FY25’s highest financial score is Biocon at 25/100, LOW; absence of a high FY25 band is a result of the supplied data and thresholds, not proof of universally low sector risk. Earlier periods show different stress and must be discussed alongside recoveries. Historical alerts are rule counts, not the number of distressed companies.

After removing qualitative influence, the October 2026 snapshot has 18 WATCH and one ENHANCED MONITORING entry. Stale financial data contributes to WATCH; Mankind’s historical HIGH financial alert supports enhanced monitoring. No active qualitative index or qualitative-based escalation remains.

Important limits: the selected sample is not the whole sector; monetary metadata/statement scope are unconfirmed; FY26 financials are missing; FY20 growth is absent; qualitative narratives include unverified/stale claims; approved limits/exposures are missing; small peer groups are heterogeneous; thresholds are policy assumptions rather than validated defaults. Explain how additional sources would strengthen the analysis, not just list limitations.
